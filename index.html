Strength Climbing Guide Summary

Key insights on Arm-Lifting & Finger Strength Training

Why Arm-Lifting / Block Pulls?

Arm-lifting allows precise progressive overloading of finger flexor tendons without overloading shoulders or spinal axial loading associated with traditional hangboard weight belts.

The "Weak Day" Principle

Connective tissue takes significantly longer to adapt and recover than muscular tissue. On low-readiness days, applying a 10% load reduction maintains mechanical tension stimulus while preventing pulley ligament strain and technique breakdown.

Grip Style Specificity

Different grips recruit distinct muscle-tendon units. Selecting your grip automatically applies its relative force capacity ratio to scale working loads appropriately.

How to Test Your 1RM (Block Pull)

Standardized protocol for accurate load calculations

1. Thorough Warm-Up (10-15 min)

Perform finger flexor gliding, light edge pulls, and body mobility. Never test 1RM cold.

2. Progressive Loading Protocol
  • 5 reps @ ~50% estimated 1RM (Rest 2 min)
  • 3 reps @ ~70% estimated 1RM (Rest 2.5 min)
  • 1 rep @ ~85% estimated 1RM (Rest 3 min)
  • 1RM Attempts: Single pulls holding 3-5 seconds with 3-4 minutes rest between attempts. Increase by 0.5kg - 2kg increments until max clean effort.
3. Valid Lift Criteria & Failure Rules

The lift counts ONLY if you break the weight clearly off the floor and hold it stationary for at least 3 continuous seconds without:

  • Jerked motion using hip or back extension momentum.
  • Slipping into a chisel/drag grip if testing Strict Half Crimp.
  • Finger joint collapse or skin slippage.
